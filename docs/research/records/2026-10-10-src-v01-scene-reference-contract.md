## 2. Which disocclusion inputs are valid?

**Verified / Upstream claim:** Motion vectors describe the displacement of a *current surface point* to the position of **the same** point in the previous frame. AMD recommends MV output for **all covered pixels** of opaque geometry; the FSR4 guide requires jitter-free MV when the designated jitter-cancellation flag is not set. [AMD FSR4 guide, ‘Providing motion vectors / Space / Coverage’](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/60f4ea81909200d8542eca14dccb2628b763a9a3/Kits/FidelityFX/docs/techniques/super-resolution-ml.md); [Cauldron GBuffer shader](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/60f4ea81909200d8542eca14dccb2628b763a9a3/Kits/Cauldron2/dx12/rendermodules/gbuffer/shaders/gbufferps.hlsl).

**Inference – precise scene definition:** In each frame `t`, at **the same jittered sample position**, the nearest surface in z is selected from an opaque foreground `F` at `z_F` and a stationary background `B` at `z_B>z_F` that covers the entire viewport. **Colour, depth and MV all come from this one visible hit.** For the current hit `P_t` and the corresponding material/object position in the previous frame `P_{t-1}`, the following holds in the agreed top-down UV system:

\[
\mathbf m_t(\mathbf x)=\operatorname{UV}_{t-1}^{\rm unjittered}(P_{t-1})-\operatorname{UV}_{t}^{\rm unjittered}(P_t).
\]

The projection/object transformations for `t−1` and `t` are geometrically consistent; **jitter is removed from both projected positions**, rather than included as image motion. The current **visible** surface determines the MV value, rather than the surface that happened to occupy the same pixel coordinate in the previous frame.

* **Previously occluded, now visible background:** With a fixed camera and stationary background, `m_B=(0,0)`, even if the previous-frame reprojection hits a *foreground pixel*. This denotes a disocclusion (an invalid history correspondence), **not an invalid current MV**. With a **moving camera**, a static background generally does **not** have `(0,0)`, but the MV correctly determined from its previous/current projections.
* **Outside the image in the previous frame:** If the current surface point has a defined previous-frame position beyond `[0,1]^2`, its reprojection remains geometrically calculable. Do not clamp to `[0,1]`, substitute the foreground MV or use an invented ‘invalid motion’ sentinel. The source may lie outside the history texture; **how the provider handles this is not publicly guaranteed**. This case specifically **cannot** occur with the fixed camera and a static background covering the entire viewport; separate camera motion or object entry is required.
* **Actual surface motion:** If a visible opaque rectangle moves by `(+d_x,+d_y)` screen pixels per frame, then for an unchanged camera and translation parallel to the image plane, `m_F=(-d_x/W,-d_y/H)` in the stored UV units; background `m_B=0`. For non-uniform motion/rotation, project each point individually instead. A newly appearing surface created without a previous-frame correspondence does **not** become a valid tracking case through arbitrarily assigned MV: **exclude** it from the minimal test or treat it separately as an explicit history discontinuity.

**Inference / open question:** Public sources specify no special ‘invalid MV’ value for disocclusions. Detecting stale history and handling off-screen reprojections are algorithm/provider responsibilities; this implies no guarantee of artefact-free disocclusion.

## 3. Device depth, samples sharing the same jitter and edges

**Verified / Upstream claim:** `ffxDispatchDescUpscale` expects current render colour, 32-bit depth, 2D MV, render/output sizes, and `cameraNear`, `cameraFar`, vertical FOV, jitter and a view-space→metres factor. Alongside the recommended reverse-Z, the FSR4 guide also supports **standard finite depth** through the appropriate creation flags. All render-resolution inputs **except MV** should be rendered with jitter. [Public header, `ffxDispatchDescUpscale`/flags](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/60f4ea81909200d8542eca14dccb2628b763a9a3/Kits/FidelityFX/upscalers/include/ffx_upscale.h); [FSR4 guide, ‘Input resources / Depth buffer configurations’](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/60f4ea81909200d8542eca14dccb2628b763a9a3/Kits/FidelityFX/docs/techniques/super-resolution-ml.md).

**Inference – an explicit, verifiable D3D projection:** Choose a perspective **LH/+Z-forward** camera, view depth `z>0`, `0<n<z<f<∞`, D3D depth `[0,1]` with `0` near/`1` far. Then

\[
d(z)=\frac{f}{f-n}-\frac{fn}{(f-n)z}=\frac{f(z-n)}{z(f-n)}.
\]

The formula follows from the specified perspective projection convention and **is not a depth encoding taken from the FSR header**. For a reproducible recommendation: `n=0.5 m`, `f=20 m`, `z_F=2 m`, `z_B=8 m`, vertical FOV `π/3`, `viewSpaceToMetersFactor=1`. These yield `d_F≈0.76923077` and `d_B≈0.96153846`; both are standard finite device depths (R32 quantization is separate). In the harness, the CPU projection must demonstrably use the same `z`, handedness, clip convention and camera parameters. A constant value of `0.5` for surfaces at different depths would be **wrong**.

**Inference – sampling rule:** With render coordinates at pixel centres `(x+0.5,y+0.5)` and **explicitly specified** top-down jitter `j_t`, a synthetic convention may use `q_t=(x+0.5−j_{x,t}, y+0.5−j_{y,t})`. `q_t` is the shared *unjittered* position of the sample actually rendered with jitter; the dispatch receives the same **defined pixel jitter** `j_t`, rather than its arbitrarily negated counterpart. The direction `−j` in this CPU mapping is a **scene/projection convention** that must be calibrated once during the later spike against a small geometrical projection example and the sample. The visibility hit is determined from `q_t`; colour and depth are read and its MV calculated **at the same hit**. Rectangle/background boundaries follow the identical projection and depth test. At the point sample, the nearest *opaque* surface applies; do not introduce an unjustified blended colour with only one unambiguous depth and the MV of another surface.

**Verified, but context-dependent:** The FSR4 guide specifies `(+2j_x/W,−2j_y/H)` for its example projection matrix; the DX12 sample callback passes `(-2j_x/W,+2j_y/H)` to its camera system. The **different signs in two interfaces** can be documented, but are **not** proof of a defect without checking the respective matrix/callback conventions; this makes a combined test of actual image displacement and dispatch jitter all the more important. [FSR4 guide, ‘Camera jitter’](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/60f4ea81909200d8542eca14dccb2628b763a9a3/Kits/FidelityFX/docs/techniques/super-resolution-ml.md); [DX12 sample, `CameraJitterCallback`](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/60f4ea81909200d8542eca14dccb2628b763a9a3/Samples/Upscalers/FidelityFX_FSR/dx12/fsrapirendermodule.cpp).

**Requirement versus recommendation:** Consistent visibility/projection and matching jittered render inputs are required for a **meaningful test**; the camera/geometry values above and **single-sample point coverage** instead of MSAA are our own simple **test choices**. The FSR4 guide declares Reactive and T&C **optional** for FSR4. An exclusively **opaque**, depth-writing rectangle moving correctly over an opaque background therefore lies **within the documented resource scope without additional masks and with flags `0`**. This suitability guarantees no particular edge quality. A more demanding MSAA/alpha/transparency/animated-texture scene would require new agreements and possibly masks/flags, and is outside the first V01 baseline. [FSR4 guide, ‘Optional resources’](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/60f4ea81909200d8542eca14dccb2628b763a9a3/Kits/FidelityFX/docs/techniques/super-resolution-ml.md); [supplementary FSR3 guide, ‘Reactive mask / Transparency and composition mask’](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/60f4ea81909200d8542eca14dccb2628b763a9a3/Kits/FidelityFX/docs/techniques/super-resolution-upscaler.md).

## 4. Motion, MV units and jitter over longer sequences

**Verified:** With standard render-resolution MV input and flags `0`, MV are at render resolution. Upstream expects pixel-space MV with a range on the order of `±(W,H)` and scales other input units using `motionVectorScale`. For **stored UV displacement** `previousUV−currentUV`, `motionVectorScale=(W,H)` is therefore dimensionally correct: `m_pixel=(W m_u,H m_v)`. +X points right, +Y downwards in top-down UV. In the GBuffer shader, `previousNDC−currentNDC` is converted to UV using `(0.5,−0.5)` only after jitter compensation. [FSR4 guide, ‘Providing motion vectors’](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/60f4ea81909200d8542eca14dccb2628b763a9a3/Kits/FidelityFX/docs/techniques/super-resolution-ml.md); [Cauldron `GBuffer.MotionVectors`](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/60f4ea81909200d8542eca14dccb2628b763a9a3/Kits/Cauldron2/dx12/rendermodules/gbuffer/shaders/gbufferps.hlsl).

**Source inconsistency (Verified):** The **code example in the FSR4 guide's explanatory text** forms raw `previousNDC−currentNDC` but combines it directly with `motionVectorScale=(W,H)`. For genuinely **raw NDC vectors**, the factor `(0.5,−0.5)` is missing; this produces a factor of two in X and an additional sign error in Y relative to top-down UV. The **official GBuffer sample explicitly corrects this**. Consequence: **do not** copy the incomplete code from the explanatory text as the harness formula. Either transform `NDC→UV` first and then scale by `(W,H)`, or explicitly derive and verify a mathematically equivalent scaling. This is a **documentation inconsistency**, not an identified Rust wrapper discrepancy.

**Verified / Upstream claim:** AMD provides a **Halton-[2,3]** jitter sequence; values are **render-pixel offsets**. For 2× scaling (‘Performance’), the guide specifies **32 phases**; other listed presets: Quality 18, Balanced 23, Ultra Performance 72; for Custom, `ceil(8n²)`. The offsets actually used for projection must be supplied in the dispatch **for each frame**. For a custom sequence, AMD recommends avoiding the zero vector `(0,0)`. [FSR4 guide, ‘Camera jitter’](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/60f4ea81909200d8542eca14dccb2628b763a9a3/Kits/FidelityFX/docs/techniques/super-resolution-ml.md); [DX12 sample, query calls `GetJitterPhaseCount`/`GetJitterOffset`](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/60f4ea81909200d8542eca14dccb2628b763a9a3/Samples/Upscalers/FidelityFX_FSR/dx12/fsrapirendermodule.cpp).

**Inference / test choice:** For the existing `320×180 → 640×360` scaling, **96 frames = 3×32 phases** is a sensible *proposed* comparison, **not** an AMD minimum duration or convergence guarantee. Use the same reproducibly stored 32-phase sequence for all test arms (derived from a documented procedure or queried once from upstream and retained as values **in the harness dataset**; do not assume a new public wrapper API). Store the jitter index, the `j_x,j_y` actually used and all renderer/dispatch parameters **for each frame**. Test horizontal `(+d_x,0)`, vertical `(0,+d_y)` and subpixel/diagonal displacement separately; the corresponding planar surface MV are `(-d_x/W,-d_y/H)` **without** the difference `j_{t−1}−j_t`. Do not round subpixel velocities to whole render pixels. Do not introduce hidden velocity/phase jumps at frame transitions, cycle boundaries or sign changes.

**Further conventions to record:** `frameTimeDelta` in **milliseconds** (`1000/60 ms` as a test proposal), `preExposure=1`, exposure texture `1.0`, constant Near/Far/FOV/metres values and flags `0`; current input colours **linear**. Record the exact quantization of `R16G16` MV at slow motion separately. [Header, dispatch fields](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/60f4ea81909200d8542eca14dccb2628b763a9a3/Kits/FidelityFX/upscalers/include/ffx_upscale.h); [FSR4 guide, ‘Exposure / Frame Time Delta Input’](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/60f4ea81909200d8542eca14dccb2628b763a9a3/Kits/FidelityFX/docs/techniques/super-resolution-ml.md).

## 5. The reference is not the upscaler algorithm

**Inference – separate mathematical objects:** Define a continuous, **unjittered** image function `I_t(s_x,s_y)` from the camera, the nearest opaque surface in z and its linear material colour. From this, generate separately:

1. **Render input:** At each render-pixel centre, evaluate `I_t(x+0.5−j_x,y+0.5−j_y)` using **the same** depth/visibility rule, and only then round each channel to `R16G16B16A16_FLOAT`. Depth from `d(z)` is separately quantized to `R32_FLOAT`, MV to `R16G16_FLOAT`. This quantized input is the actual render resource to be checked; an analytical double/float value is **not** automatically its stored value.
2. **Independent display point reference:** At 2×, for display pixel `(X,Y)`, `I_t((X+0.5)/2,(Y+0.5)/2)` is the unjittered **point sample**. Do not apply jitter a second time to the reference; do not interpolate quantized low-resolution colour back up and present it as ground truth.
3. **Optional pixel-area reference:** `R^{area}_t(X,Y)=\frac{1}{A}\int_{A_{XY}}I_t(s)\,ds` in the **render coordinate system**, with display-pixel area `A_{XY}` of size `(1/2)×(1/2)` for 2×. At silhouettes, this produces an exact **coverage blend**. An area-integrated ideal and a point sample differ **precisely at hard edges**; the choice between them is a **measurement objective**, not an FSR4 API guarantee.
4. **Upscaler reconstruction:** The signed provider's output `U_t` processes current quantized low-resolution inputs and potentially several previous frames. **Do not** postulate that `U_t` must exactly produce the point sample or area integral. Both references permit **descriptive** error analyses with an explicit target image, rather than a universal statement of correctness.

**Specific sources of reference error:** Confusing low-resolution half-float with ideal 64-bit colour; rounding `R16G16` MV differently before versus after scaling; treating `R32_FLOAT` device depth as linear `z/f`; using the opposite projection-jitter sign or forgetting half-pixel offsets; combining foreground colour with background depth at edges; assuming clamping/sample filtering at the display boundary when the analytical function is defined differently there; using a different visibility time/phase in the analytical reference image; comparing in sRGB/gamma rather than linear space; silently presenting output half-float readbacks as ideal real values. Any of these discrepancies can make a **reference/harness error appear to be an upscaler error**.

**Inference – sound error attribution:** In addition to the high-quality independent display reference, retain an **input sanity record**: numerical sample locations, expected object ID, analytical/quantized colour, depth and MV, jitter, camera, visibility at `t`/`t−1`; where appropriate, check reprojection `p+m` against analytical previous-frame geometry. Without a known reconstruction procedure, there is **no** ‘ideal upscaler output corrected for half-float’. The difference between two clearly defined oracles (point versus area), input quantization and spatial aliasing limits must be reported separately in the interpretation. **No source establishes a universal MAE/RMSE/ghosting threshold.**

## 6. Reset: documented purpose, but no output-identity guarantee

**Verified:** The public dispatch header describes `reset` as indicating **discontinuous camera movement**; the FSR4 guide requires `reset=true` for the **first frame of the new camera state at a jump cut**. Other types of history discontinuity are **not exhaustively catalogued** there. [Header, `reset`](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/60f4ea81909200d8542eca14dccb2628b763a9a3/Kits/FidelityFX/upscalers/include/ffx_upscale.h); [FSR4 guide, ‘Camera jump cuts’](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/60f4ea81909200d8542eca14dccb2628b763a9a3/Kits/FidelityFX/docs/techniques/super-resolution-ml.md).

**Verified, FSR3 implementation only:** The open FSR3 host function uses `params->reset || context->firstExecution` as the accumulation-reset condition, manages resources dependent on frame parity, among other things, and initializes `resourceFrameIndex=0` at context creation. This **explains** the test comparison, but makes **no binding statement** about signed FSR4 reset internals, state clearing or bit identity. [Open FSR3 host implementation, `fsr3upscalerDispatch`, `resetAccumulation`, `resourceFrameIndex`](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/60f4ea81909200d8542eca14dccb2628b763a9a3/Kits/FidelityFX/upscalers/fsr3/internal/ffx_fsr3upscaler.cpp).

**Inference / recommendation:** Retain `reset=true` on frame `0` as a controlled **initialization strategy**, but **do not** declare it an explicit FSR4 requirement for fresh contexts. For a camera cut `t_c`: `reset=true` at `t_c`, then from `t_c+1` use `false` again if the new scene is continuous. Include further *clearly defined* history discontinuities (e.g. teleportation of a scene object whose previous-frame correspondence no longer exists, an abrupt projection/scene change) only with a justified reset-hypothesis test; no unrecorded discontinuities.

**Pairing ‘history reset’ versus ‘fresh context’:** The same *current frame* with **identical resource bytes** (colour, depth, MV, exposure), render/output sizes, flags, preExposure, CameraNear/Far/FOV/Scale, `frameTimeDelta`, `jitterOffset`, sharpening setting and reset value. Identical provider/runtime/GPU/driver versions, identical submission/ordering and full GPU fences; the same planned jitter and motion sample, with no implicit reset of the **scene** when creating a new context. The two contexts differ **only** in prior context history/creation time. Compare the current reset frame and, where applicable, subsequent frames separately.

**Unresolved:** **Neither bit-exact equality nor a particular numerical tolerance is a public FSR4 guarantee.** ‘`reset` makes the output approximately equal to the first frame of a fresh context’ is a **plausible test hypothesis**; an observed tolerance band becomes a **local** acceptance criterion only after repeated provider calibration and independent replication. Frame parity/hidden state/differing resource initialization remain possible confounders.

## 7. Small measurement suite and negative controls

**Recommendation – in the spike, evaluate all three RGB channels per frame, not just red**, separately for smooth interior regions with stable references, continuously visible background, currently covered foreground, edge bands and *newly revealed background*. Object/visibility masks are generated **independently** from the analytical geometry at **display resolution**. Reference type (point or area), linear colour-space domain, any boundary exclusion and pixel/frame counting are defined in advance and reported.

| Comparison | Specific definition / purpose | Status of the expectation |
|---|---|---|
| **Spatial error** | Per region and frame, RGB-MAE, RGB-RMSE, selected quantiles/maximum against the **named** display reference; separate from input validation. Optional PSNR only with a specified peak and without implicit clipping. | **Test metric**, no universal threshold. |
| **Residual temporal fluctuation** | For stationary, continuously visible pixels, `e_t=U_t−R_t`, distribution of `|e_t−e_{t−1}|`; for moving surfaces **visible in both frames**, evaluate along the *correct ground-truth flow reprojection*. Also check phase separation `t↔t+32` in a static scene. | **Test hypothesis**: correct inputs should produce less unjustified flicker; no monotonicity guarantee. |
| **Disocclusion/ghosting** | Newly revealed BG mask: `B` at `t`, but `F` at the reprojected BG position in `t−1`. Analyse error by **age since revelation** (0, 1, 2–5 frames) and distance from the current silhouette (e.g. `0–2`, `2–8`, `>8` **display** pixels). With constant, well-separated `C_F,C_B`, also report the signed **foreground leakage score** `g=((U−C_B)·(C_F−C_B))/||C_F−C_B||²` in a region known to contain only BG. | **Diagnostic**; directly at the AA edge, coverage/filter behaviour must be considered; `g=0` is not a universally guaranteed acceptance gate. |
| **Reset pair** | Same current-frame inputs: `history+reset` versus `fresh+reset`; MAE/RMSE/maximum deviation per region and as a full readback. Use a missing-reset control for a cut after substantial history. | **Hypothesis**; calibrate numerical values first. |

A **global mean** can dilute a narrow edge trail crucial to ghosting with very many simple background pixels. Even a low global RMSE does not distinguish a visually acceptable edge-antialiasing difference from a prolonged coloured trail. Therefore retain **regions, quantiles/maxima, the time axis and example pixel traces**; document the first/last frame position, jitter phase and any clipping effects.

**Targeted negative controls, each using the same correct frame/jitter input except for the stated manipulation:** (a) **invert only foreground MV-X** for horizontal motion, and likewise Y for vertical motion; negating the stationary background's `(0,0)` would not be an effective control arm; (b) freeze foreground MV at `0` or the previous-frame value despite motion; (c) negate/shift by one phase **only the jitter reported in the dispatch**, leaving the colour/depth actually generated with jitter unchanged; (d) after an established camera cut, `reset=false` instead of `true`, and alternatively, in a continuous scene, incorrectly use `reset=true` every frame. Controls may produce effects of different magnitudes; **correct < incorrect** for suitable ghosting/temporal metrics is a **sensitivity hypothesis to be tested**, not a guaranteed ranking. Large erroneous resets need not necessarily worsen single-frame RMSE. Each negative variant is deliberately **not a valid positive input** and must not be assessed as evidence of an upscaler regression.

## 10. Minimal V01 scene proposal — **recommendation, not an architecture decision**

**Profile:** As in the baseline, **no** new flag/no new public API; `W×H=320×180`, output `640×360` (2×), linear RGB range `[0,1]`, opaque, Exposure=`1.0`, preExposure=`1.0`, `frameTimeDelta=1000/60 ms`, sharpening off as a **test choice** to isolate effects, reset on frame 0. LH perspective +Z, `n=0.5`, `f=20`, `fovY=π/3`, metres factor `1`, foreground `z_F=2`, background `z_B=8` with device depth calculated from these values. A flat opaque rectangular foreground surface (e.g. projected size 64×44 render pixels) moves smoothly over a stationary background plane covering the entire viewport, with a subdued analytical pattern and a constant contrasting region behind the silhouette. Material/object IDs remain persistent. For **every** jittered render sample in every frame: select the nearest surface in z; calculate its linear colour, depth and unjittered previous–current MV; then quantize to the agreed DXGI formats.

**Proposed sequential test cases (separate contexts/arms, no runtime results):** (1) 96 frames of a stationary background plane, cycling through 32 jitter phases, as a zero-motion/temporal reference. (2) 96 frames each of horizontal, vertical and subpixel-diagonal foreground translation, e.g. velocities `(+1.25,0)`, `(0,+0.5)` and `(+0.375,+0.25)` **render pixels/frame**. Choose starting positions/size so that the foreground remains entirely within the viewport for at least the first three cycles; do not reset motion at phase 32. (3) A separate 96-frame camera-cut pair, with the cut e.g. before frame 48: correct `reset=true` in the cut frame, a negative control without reset, and a **fresh context from that exact cut frame** with the identical current scene. (4) **Optional, clearly separate**: continuous camera displacement such that a visible background surface previously projected outside the view; derive MV for both foreground and background from the full camera reprojection, rather than universally assigning zero or identical MV values. This optional off-screen scenario requires no new flags but extends scene/matrix validation.

**Fix in advance of the later native run:** Per frame, scene ID, position/velocity, camera/projection/depth formula, jitter table and phase actually used, exposure/flags/reset, quantized input bytes, analytical visibility and disocclusion masks, point and optionally area-integrated display reference, readback format, evaluation domain, ROI/band/age definitions and control-arm diff. Retain per-frame submission/fencing as a **harness strategy**, not a general synchronization guarantee. Retain full input/output readbacks and provenance (GPU/driver/provider/SDK/crates/hashes) during the spike.

## 11. Specific questions for the native calibration spike

1. Does the **input-only** sanity test on actual `R16/R32` resources reproduce camera, jitter and depth values at representative interior and boundary pixels (including quantization)? Do the object ID, C/Z/MV at the silhouette pixel and reprojected point coordinates agree?
2. Does the chosen jitter-sign/matrix path produce **measured** sample displacements in X/Y consistent with the pixel offsets reported to dispatch? What happens when the callback/matrix sign changes?
3. Do correct versus deliberately inverted, frozen or missing **foreground** MV reproducibly distinguish the disoccluded ghosting region and the continuously visible surfaces? What effects do quantization and subpixel velocities have?
4. How do spatial error and residual temporal fluctuation change over **32, 64, 96 frames**? Do results depend on jitter phase, warm-up state, motion speed, direction or incidental frame parity? Are there stable local error distributions or only non-monotonic effects?
5. How do **point** and **area references** differ precisely at the silhouette? Do interior/disocclusion findings remain robust to the choice of oracle? What proportion arises from input half-float quantization?
6. How are errors distributed in the background **when first revealed** and after 1/2–5 frames compared with permanently visible background? Does the old foreground produce measurable leakage in its colour direction; how long does it remain visible?
7. Is the cut frame for `history+reset` and `fresh+reset` on this hardware/provider/runtime **bit-identical, numerically close or clearly different**? Does this change with different cut frames, jitter phases, context lifetimes and in subsequent frames?
8. Does incorrectly **omitting reset** at a cut lead to statistically detectable artefacts? Does **resetting every frame** produce different (not necessarily strictly worse) temporal metrics than proper accumulation?
9. How does the provider handle **correct** reprojection to previous-frame UV outside `[0,1]^2` compared with true disocclusion within the view? Can both be observed reliably and separately without a custom sentinel convention and without changing the API scope?
10. Which **numerical criteria** (per ROI, quantile, time window, reference type) can be established reliably from **calibration runs** with archived readbacks, and are they reproduced in **separate verification runs kept unchanged thereafter**? Do not reinterpret or overwrite results from earlier smoke/sharpening checks in the process.

## 12. Limits of the findings

This source record examines **public sources**, not `fsr-sdk-rs` code or the DLL's quality on a GPU. The pinned SDK commit identifies the source; the specific **signed** FSR4 provider selected at runtime cannot be inferred from open FSR3 source code. All proposed frames, motions, RMSE/ghosting metrics, ROI sizes and error expectations are **test designs/hypotheses**, not AMD-mandated thresholds. **Calibration** to define criteria and **later verification** against frozen criteria are strictly separate activities. SDK upgrades, new public APIs, HDR/auto-exposure/mask implementation, general in-flight/threading claims, GBV stall diagnosis and a new sharpening-effect gate remain excluded.
