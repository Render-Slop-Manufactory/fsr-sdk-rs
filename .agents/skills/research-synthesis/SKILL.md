# Research Synthesis

Read [shared research guidance](../RESEARCH.md). Identify supplied records and the
requested topic; if scope is unspecified, inspect new or unreferenced research
records without reprocessing all historical material.

Review incoming content and artefacts for secrets or personal information.
Normalize new records to the shared naming convention, preserving substantive
content and provenance. Do not rename established records for cosmetic reasons.

Read the affected synthesis and prior evidence needed to assess differences.
Compare versions and scope before declaring agreement or contradiction. Integrate
findings into a concise current view, retaining qualifications, disagreements,
source links, and questions requiring experiments. Link every included record;
reuse a canonical record across topics instead of copying it.

Create a topic synthesis and research index only when needed for actual incoming
evidence. Keep index entries short. Update broader status or decision documents
only when the task authorizes that change and the evidence supports it; a
recommendation alone is not an accepted decision or completed implementation.

Check links, dates, evidence provenance, and that every requested input was
accounted for. Report the updated topics and material unresolved conflicts.
